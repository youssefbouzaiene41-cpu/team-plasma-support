Child labour: key facts

Child labour means work that harms children's health, safety, development or education, or prevents them from attending school.

7.9%

National estimate

About 7.9% of children aged 5–17 in Tunisia were reported to be in child labour in the 2017 national survey.

5–17

Age group

The statistic refers to children aged 5 to 17 years old.

School

Education matters

Child labour can make it harder for children to attend school, learn and build a better future.

Important: The percentage above is a national estimate from 2017, not a current 2026 figure. Reliable, comparable figures for each of Kairouan, Kasserine and Sidi Bouzid are not included here, so we do not invent them.

Causes and consequences

Possible causes

  • Poverty and financial pressure
  • Limited access to quality education
  • Family or community expectations
  • Lack of decent work for adults

Possible consequences

  • Missing school or leaving early
  • Injury, illness or exhaustion
  • Stress and loss of free time
  • Fewer opportunities in adulthood

What can we do?

🏫 Support education

Encourage regular school attendance and help children access learning materials.

🤝 Raise awareness

Share reliable information and talk about children's rights respectfully.

🛡️ Protect children

Support safe ways to report situations where a child may be at risk.

This is an educational school project. It does not collect donations or process payments.

About this project

Team Plasma Support is a student awareness project about child labour in Tunisia. Its message is simple: every child deserves education, protection and time to grow.

Project motto: Protect childhood. Support education. Build hope.
